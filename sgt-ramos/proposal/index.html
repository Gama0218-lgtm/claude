Case theory

The legal argument in one sentence

When a researcher uses the government’s own databases — audited by the government’s own preferred methodology — the findings are not claims against the government. They are the government’s data, cross-examined.

Legal frame

Why it looks defensible

  • BISG is the CFPB’s fair-lending methodology. Applying it to DCAS uses the government’s own tool against the government’s own file.
  • PSID is federally funded. Findings derived from it are standard social science, not advocacy.
  • FOIA-disclosed records carry the government’s implicit authentication.
  • Multi-source verification (CMOHS + The Wall + Coffelt + BIRLS) meets the standard for historical corroboration.
  • Structural silence in a federal database is evidentiary: it documents erasure, not absence.
Grant frame

Why it looks fundable

  • LITCENTRAL + TruthEngine360 is a replicable methodology community researchers can deploy using federal public data.
  • The manuscript is preservation-ready with documented methodology and benchmarks — not conceptually vague.
  • The project links literary form, public history, Chicano studies, digital humanities, and veteran policy.
  • 21 Omega Elite chapters represent a quantified quality tier. The math is shown.
  • Editing, PSID data access, FOIA filing, and open-access publication are concrete, fundable deliverables.
