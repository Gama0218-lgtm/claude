Subject: BIRLS Records — Non-Citizen Vietnam Veterans; XC-File Policy
         Documents; Benefits Denial Records


Dear VA FOIA Service Officer:

This letter constitutes a formal escalation of my Freedom of Information Act
request originally submitted on [ORIGINAL VA FOIA DATE], assigned Tracking
Number [VA TRACKING NUMBER].


I. STATUTORY NOTICE OF OVERDUE RESPONSE

Under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(A)(i), the Department of Veterans Affairs was
required to determine whether to comply with my request within twenty (20)
working days of receipt. Based on the submission date of [ORIGINAL VA FOIA
DATE], that statutory deadline expired on approximately [CALCULATED
DEADLINE — 20 business days from original request date].

As of the date of this letter, I have received no substantive response —
no rolling disclosure, no Vaughn index, no estimated completion date, and
no written explanation for the delay beyond any initial acknowledgment.

Under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(C)(i), a requester may treat an agency's
failure to respond within the statutory period as a constructive denial and
proceed directly to district court without exhausting administrative appeals.
I am submitting this escalation letter before initiating litigation solely
as a matter of administrative courtesy, and to provide your office one
final opportunity to comply with the statutory requirements.


II. RECORDS REQUESTED

I request production of the following records in their entirety, or, where
exemptions apply, with non-exempt portions segregated and produced:

1. BIRLS RECORDS — NAMED INDIVIDUALS
   All entries in the Beneficiary Identification and Records Locator
   Subsystem (BIRLS) for the individuals identified in Attachment A,
   including: service period and branch, separation code, character of
   discharge, claim file number, beneficiary status, citizenship or alien
   status notation, and any administrative flag affecting benefits
   eligibility. [NOTE: Attachment A is your list of MNV-01 through MNV-15
   veterans — include names and, if available, Social Security Numbers or
   VA file numbers. Redact SSNs from the version you retain in public
   files.]

2. XC-FILE POLICY DOCUMENTS
   All internal VA directives, policy memoranda, training circulars,
   guidance documents, and correspondence issued from January 1, 1970
   through the present date regarding the processing, adjudication, or
   denial of benefits claims filed by non-citizen veterans or veterans
   with pending or completed immigration proceedings — including any
   documents referencing "alien claims," "XC-files," or benefits
   eligibility conditioned on citizenship or lawful permanent resident
   status. This request specifically includes any policy documents issued
   or revised between January 1, 2024 and the present.

3. BENEFITS DENIAL RECORDS — NON-CITIZEN VIETNAM-ERA VETERANS
   Aggregate or de-identified records reflecting the number, type, and
   stated grounds for benefits denials issued to veterans who served
   during the Vietnam War period (August 5, 1964 – May 7, 1975) and who
   were identified at the time of application as non-citizens, lawful
   permanent residents, or individuals in immigration proceedings —
   covering the period January 1, 1975 through December 31, 2000.

4. VA-DHS COORDINATION RECORDS
   Any records — including memoranda of understanding, interagency
   agreements, emails, or correspondence — reflecting coordination between
   the Department of Veterans Affairs and the Department of Homeland
   Security (or its predecessor agencies, the Immigration and
   Naturalization Service and the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration
   Services) regarding veterans subject to deportation or removal
   proceedings, from January 1, 1990 through the present.


III. EXEMPTION OBJECTIONS

Should your agency intend to invoke any FOIA exemption, I specifically
require the following before withholding any record in whole or in part:

   (a) A Vaughn index identifying each withheld document by date, author,
       recipient, subject, and length;

   (b) The specific statutory exemption claimed for each withheld document
       and a non-conclusory factual explanation of why the exemption applies
       to that specific document;

   (c) Confirmation that all non-exempt portions of partially exempt
       documents have been segregated and produced per 5 U.S.C.
       § 552(b) (final clause).

A categorical assertion that a class of records is exempt — without
document-by-document justification — is insufficient. See Vaughn v. Rosen,
484 F.2d 820 (D.C. Cir. 1973).


IV. FEE WAIVER

I renew my request, first made in the original FOIA submission, for a full
waiver of all search, duplication, and review fees under 5 U.S.C.
§ 552(a)(4)(A)(iii). Disclosure is in the public interest because:

   (a) The requested records concern the federal government's treatment of
       veterans who served in the United States Armed Forces — a matter of
       significant and ongoing public concern;

   (b) The information will be incorporated into independent scholarly
       research and will be made publicly available through institutional
       archival deposits at the University of Southern California Libraries,
       the Library of Congress Veterans History Project, and the National
       Archives, among other repositories;

   (c) The requester is a PhD candidate in public policy at the USC Sol
       Price School of Public Policy and the Executive Director of the
       AUMER Foundation, a nonprofit organization. No commercial purpose
       is served by this request.


V. DEMAND FOR RESPONSE

I require a written response within ten (10) business days of the date of
this letter. That response must either:

   (a) Confirm that production of responsive documents has commenced and
       provide a binding rolling-production schedule with projected
       completion date; or

   (b) Provide a written explanation for the delay, identify the specific
       exceptional circumstances justifying the delay under 5 U.S.C.
       § 552(a)(6)(B), and state a binding date by which full production
       will be completed.

If I do not receive a substantive response by [DATE 10 BUSINESS DAYS FROM
SEND DATE], I will treat this matter as a constructive denial under 5
U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(C)(i) and initiate proceedings in the United States
District Court pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(B) without further notice.

Respectfully submitted,


Gabriel Arce
Executive Director, AUMER Foundation
PhD Candidate, USC Sol Price School of Public Policy
[YOUR ADDRESS]
gtarce@usc.edu
[YOUR PHONE]

                 (MNV-01 through MNV-15, with service information)

ICE FOIA Tracking Number: [ICE TRACKING NUMBER]
Subject: ENFORCE / DACS / EOIR Records — Deportation Proceedings
         Involving Individuals with Documented U.S. Military Service


Dear ICE FOIA Officer:

This letter constitutes a formal escalation of my Freedom of Information
Act request originally submitted on [ORIGINAL DHS FOIA DATE], assigned
Tracking Number [ICE TRACKING NUMBER].


I. STATUTORY NOTICE OF OVERDUE RESPONSE

Under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(A)(i), U.S. Immigration and Customs
Enforcement was required to determine whether to comply with my request
within twenty (20) working days of receipt. Based on the submission date
of [ORIGINAL DHS FOIA DATE], that statutory deadline expired on
approximately [CALCULATED DEADLINE — 20 business days from original
request date].

As of the date of this letter, I have received no substantive response.
Pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(C)(i), I am entitled to treat this
failure as a constructive denial and proceed to district court. I submit
this escalation letter before initiating litigation as a matter of
administrative process, and to provide ICE a final opportunity to comply.


II. RECORDS REQUESTED

I request production of the following records, or, where exemptions apply,
the non-exempt portions of such records segregated and produced:

1. ENFORCE / EID DATABASE RECORDS — NAMED INDIVIDUALS
   All records in the Enforcement Integrated Database (ENFORCE/EID) or
   its predecessor systems — including DACS (Deportable Alien Control
   System) and the Alien File (A-File) — for the individuals identified
   in Attachment A. Requested fields include: case initiation date,
   grounds for enforcement action, country of origin, any notation of
   military service or veteran status, removal or deportation order date,
   country of removal, and any administrative hold or prosecutorial
   discretion notation.

   NOTE: This request specifically includes records for individuals whose
   removal proceedings were initiated between 1975 and 1996 (pre-IIRIRA),
   as early deportation records are central to establishing the historical
   timeline.

2. POLICY DOCUMENTS — VETERANS AND MILITARY SERVICE
   All ICE (and predecessor INS) directives, policy memoranda, training
   materials, and prosecution guidelines issued from January 1, 1990
   through the present date regarding the exercise of prosecutorial
   discretion, enforcement priorities, or removal proceedings for
   individuals who have documented U.S. military service — including any
   policies referencing "military service," "veteran status," or the
   Parole in Place (PIP) program for veterans.

3. AGGREGATE STATISTICS — VETERAN DEPORTATIONS
   Any records reflecting the number of individuals removed or deported
   from the United States who had documented U.S. military service,
   disaggregated by fiscal year from FY1990 through FY2024, to the extent
   such aggregate records exist.

4. EOIR RECORDS — IMMIGRATION COURT PROCEEDINGS
   All records held by ICE relating to immigration court proceedings
   (Executive Office for Immigration Review) for the named individuals in
   Attachment A, including: Notice to Appear documents, charging
   documents, attorney-of-record notations, and final orders of removal.
   [Note: If EOIR records are held by DOJ rather than ICE, please so
   advise and provide the appropriate referral contact.]

5. VA COORDINATION RECORDS
   Any records reflecting coordination, referral, or information sharing
   between ICE (or predecessor INS) and the Department of Veterans
   Affairs regarding individuals who are veterans or have pending VA
   benefits claims.


III. EXEMPTION OBJECTIONS

Should ICE invoke any FOIA exemption:

   (a) A Vaughn index is required for each withheld document, identifying
       the document by date, author, subject, and length;

   (b) The specific exemption must be identified and a non-conclusory
       factual justification provided for each document withheld;

   (c) Any partial exemption must be accompanied by production of all
       non-exempt material within that document.

I specifically object to any categorical invocation of the law enforcement
exemption under 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7) without document-by-document
justification. The individuals identified in this request are subjects of
historical research, not active law enforcement targets. Any claimed
privacy exemption under § 552(b)(6) or (b)(7)(C) must be weighed against
the significant public interest in documenting the deportation of U.S.
military veterans.


IV. PUBLIC INTEREST FEE WAIVER

I renew my fee waiver request under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii):

   (a) These records document the federal government's enforcement actions
       against individuals who served in the United States Armed Forces —
       a matter of substantial public concern and ongoing congressional
       and judicial interest;

   (b) The records will be used in independent scholarly research and
       made publicly available through institutional archival deposits at
       the USC Libraries, Library of Congress, and National Archives;

   (c) The requester is a PhD candidate in public policy and Executive
       Director of the AUMER Foundation. No commercial purpose is served.


V. DEMAND FOR RESPONSE

I require a written response within ten (10) business days confirming
either:

   (a) That production of responsive documents has commenced with a
       binding rolling-production schedule; or

   (b) A written explanation for the delay identifying the exceptional
       circumstances under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(B) and a binding
       production completion date.

Absent a substantive response by [DATE 10 BUSINESS DAYS FROM SEND DATE],
I will treat this as a constructive denial under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(6)(C)(i)
and initiate district court proceedings without further notice.

Respectfully submitted,


Gabriel Arce
Executive Director, AUMER Foundation
PhD Candidate, USC Sol Price School of Public Policy

gtarce@usc.edu
