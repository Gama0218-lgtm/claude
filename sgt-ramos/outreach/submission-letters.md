The project is titled SGT George Ramos: The Mathematics of Vietnam — a
245,000-word literary-historical manuscript documenting the administrative
exclusion of Chicano and non-citizen veterans from the official record of
the Vietnam War.

THE CORE FINDING

The Defense Department's own casualty database — the Defense Casualty
Analysis System (DCAS), covering all 58,220 U.S. Vietnam casualties —
audited by the same Bayesian Improved Surname Geocoding (BISG) methodology
used by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau in fair-lending
enforcement, shows an 84.9% classification failure rate for Hispanic
casualties: 349 officially coded against an estimated 2,309–3,000.

This is not a claim against the government's database. It is a calculation
that the Department of Defense can reproduce on its own systems.

SUBMISSION CONTENTS

This archival submission includes:

  1. The complete manuscript (245,000 words; SHA-256 manifest attached)
  2. DCAS/BISG audit dataset — full surname-coded casualty analysis
     (N=58,220; R²=0.947)
  3. PSID variable registry — six longitudinal survey waves documenting
     benefits denied due to non-citizenship status, including restricted
     variable identifiers for independent verification
  4. Fifteen veteran case files (MNV-01 through MNV-15), verified across
     four independent databases at tiers T1–T4
  5. Electronic footprint manifest — PREMIS-compatible, SIP-ready
  6. Legal argument brief establishing the evidentiary methodology

WHY USC LIBRARIES

USC's Chicano Studies collection and its relationship with the Los Angeles
Vietnam-era veteran community make this the natural primary custodian for
this material. The research was conducted in part at USC's Sol Price School
using PSID restricted data; depositing the evidentiary infrastructure here
completes that institutional chain of custody.

This submission also initiates what I am calling the Electronic Footprint
Protocol — a multi-institution archival deposit strategy designed to place
the research record in independent institutional custody, ensuring that the
evidentiary findings cannot be administratively contested without engaging
the institutional verification process of each custodian.

A full evidentiary packet is available digitally and can be transmitted
via secure transfer or physical media per your protocol. I am also
prepared to present the research methodology to your collections committee.

Dear Director and Collection Staff,

I am writing to submit materials to the Veterans History Project (VHP)
for permanent archival consideration at the American Folklife Center,
Library of Congress.

The submission documents the service and post-service experiences of
fifteen Chicano and non-citizen Vietnam veterans whose administrative
records were either absent from, misclassified within, or actively
excluded from the official federal record of their service.

THE COLLECTION

The materials comprise:

  1. Fifteen verified veteran case files (MNV-01 through MNV-15) —
     cross-referenced against the Defense Casualty Analysis System,
     PSID longitudinal survey data, VA BIRLS records, and FOIA-disclosed
     service documentation. Each file is assigned a verification tier
     (T1–T4) based on the strength of the evidentiary chain.

  2. Supporting manuscript: SGT George Ramos: The Mathematics of Vietnam,
     a 245,000-word literary-historical work constructing the narrative
     context for the fifteen documented cases and the statistical finding
     that underpins them.

  3. DCAS/BISG audit dataset: A full audit of the Defense Casualty Analysis
     System (58,220 casualties) using Bayesian Improved Surname Geocoding
     — the same methodology used in federal fair-lending enforcement —
     finding an 84.9% classification failure rate for Hispanic casualties.

  4. PSID variable registry: Six longitudinal survey waves documenting
     benefits denied to non-citizen veterans, with restricted variable
     identifiers for independent replication.

  5. Electronic footprint manifest, PREMIS-compatible.

WHY THIS COLLECTION BELONGS AT THE LIBRARY OF CONGRESS

The Veterans History Project's mission is to collect and preserve the
personal narratives of America's wartime veterans. The fifteen individuals
documented in this collection did not leave oral history recordings
accessible to conventional archival collection — many were deported,
died without benefits, or were administratively invisible. What remains
of their record is a cross-verified documentary chain, constructed from
government databases that the government itself can authenticate.

Depositing these materials at VHP places them in permanent federal
custody — independent of the agencies whose records they audit — and
creates a preservation chain that no administrative revision can undo.

One case: MNV-04, a documented pre-IIRIRA deportation (1985) of a
Vietnam-era veteran whose removal predates the 1996 law that most
scholars treat as the origin of veteran deportation. The VHP collection
would be the permanent custodian of that documentation.

REQUEST

  (a) Review of the submitted materials for inclusion in the Veterans
      History Project collection;
  (b) An assigned VHP accession identifier for footprint documentation;
  (c) Guidance on your preferred submission format for complex,
      multi-source documentary collections (we have PREMIS-compatible
      metadata prepared).

A full digital packet can be transmitted via your online submission portal
or by secure transfer. I am available for a preliminary call to discuss
the collection's scope and format.

Thank you for preserving these records.

Respectfully,

Gabriel Arce
PhD Candidate, USC Sol Price School of Public Policy
Executive Director, AUMER Foundation
gtarce@usc.edu
