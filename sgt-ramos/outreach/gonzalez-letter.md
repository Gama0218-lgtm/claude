I am writing to request your scholarly expertise in connection with a 245,000-word literary-historical manuscript I have completed: *SGT George Ramos: The Mathematics of Vietnam*. The work builds directly on the tradition established by Ralph C. Guzmán — whose 1969 Spanish-surname casualty analysis across five Southwestern states found that Latinos comprised approximately 11.9% of the population but 19.4% of casualties — and extends it, fifty-five years later, to the full national DCAS record.

What we found when we applied the BIFSG methodology (Bayesian Improved First-Name Surname Geocoding, with Tzioumis 2018 first-name priors) to all 58,220 Vietnam fatalities is not what we expected. The DCAS file publishes 349 Hispanic casualties. That figure breaks down as 345 from Puerto Rico, 4 from the U.S. Virgin Islands, and zero from any of the 50 states. The number is not a count of Hispanic casualties. It is a territorial proxy — a classification artifact produced between 2000 and 2008 when DMDC retroactively applied OMB Directive 15 to wartime records that never carried an ethnicity field, substituting home-of-record territory for ethnic identity.

The BIFSG reconciliation file identifies 1,789 additional mainland Spanish-surname Vietnam KIA coded WHITE — Esqueda, Hernandez, Cisneros, Ramirez, Gomez-Gutierrez, Ruvalcaba-Lopez — each with a posterior above 0.90. Texas (711), California (640), New Mexico (148), Arizona (113). The classification failure ratio is 84.9%. Three independent estimators — BISG floor (2,309), BIFSG corridor (2,876–3,372), Project 100,000 demographic range (2,526–5,156) — all confirm the same undercount. Guzmán identified the pattern. The records confirm the mechanism.

The manuscript situates this finding within fifteen verified veteran case files (MNV-01 through MNV-15), cross-referenced across military records, VA BIRLS data, and family testimony at four verification tiers. Among them: a pre-IIRIRA deportation case from 1985 (Manuel Segura, Bronze Star), a Medal of Honor recipient with a 37-year recognition gap (SPC4 Jesús S. Durán, born Juárez), and a veteran deported in 2025 who publicly confronted a sitting DHS Secretary.
