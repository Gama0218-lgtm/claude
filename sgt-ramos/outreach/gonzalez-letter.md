I am writing to request your scholarly expertise in connection with a 245,000-word literary-historical manuscript I have completed: *SGT George Ramos: The Mathematics of Vietnam*. The work documents the life of Sergeant George Ramos — an Army A-8 soldier and personal friend — and situates his story within a larger statistical and historical framework that has received no sustained academic attention: the systematic misclassification of Hispanic casualties in the Defense Casualty Analysis System (DCAS) and the Bureau of the Census's own records from the Vietnam era.

An audit I conducted against BISG (Bayesian Improved Surname Geocoding) methodology reveals an 84.9% Hispanic casualty classification failure rate in DCAS records — approximately 349 casualties coded where demographic modeling suggests between 2,309 and 3,000 Hispanic servicemen died, out of a total casualty universe of N=58,220. This is not an interpretive gap. It is a structural omission that has shaped how we understand who bore the weight of that war.

The manuscript operates at the intersection of literary narrative, quantitative analysis, and archival research. Its fifteen veteran case files (MNV-01 through MNV-15) are verified through military records, VA BIRLS data, and family testimony, cross-referenced across four verification tiers.
